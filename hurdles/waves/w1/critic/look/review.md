## Verdict (not blind)

The reference wins clearly: it reads as a finished game in every still, while ours reads as a tech-art demo whose lighting and post pass are actively fighting the scene rather than selling it.

## Biggest gap

The torch "lighting" is not real light on the voxels — it is a flat, screen-space decal painted under everything else, and the default post pass (dither + haze) makes the image worse, not better. Proof: the showcase's own debug keys (`1` post, `4` dither, `6` haze) let you strip the pipeline apart. With `1` (post) off, every torch's warm glow disappears from the walls entirely — the brick strip spanning from one torch, past the unlit archway, to the next torch is a uniform flat brown-dark tone with **no brightness falloff at all** tied to torch distance (compare `/tmp/look-inspect/wallstrip-postoff.png` to `/tmp/look-inspect/wallstrip-on.png`, or just toggle `1` in `?showcase=look` yourself). The soft round light pools you see by default are a 2D gradient stamped on top of an otherwise flatly-shaded scene, not a point light interacting with wall/floor normals. On top of that, the default dither+haze pass desaturates and flattens everything it touches (compare `toggles/baseline.png` to `toggles/post_off.png` in the capture set below) — richer torch color, cleaner floor, better-separated cage bars, crisper outlines, all with post off. Fix: replace the decal with a real point light that shades wall and floor voxels by surface normal and distance (so it wraps correctly onto vertical faces, not just the floor plane), and either cut the dither/haze opacity drastically or drop it — the must_have asks for "flickering torch light that actually lights nearby voxels," and right now it doesn't.

## Problems

1. **Torch light doesn't touch nearby voxels (must_have violation).** See "Biggest gap" above. Evidence: `?showcase=look`, press `1` to toggle post off/on and watch the wall brick strip between two torches — with post off there is no falloff at all. Screenshots: `hurdles/waves/w1/critic/look/ours/03-lit-pool-detail.png` (floor decal, note the wall directly behind gets nothing) vs the reference's `ceaa35df`-style light pools, which read as real illumination because NT bakes them into the tile art *and* keeps the surrounding geometry legible.

2. **The default post pass (dither + haze) makes the scene worse than the raw render.** Toggling `4` (dither) and `6` (haze) off, or `1` (post) off entirely, produces a cleaner, richer, more contrasty image every time — richer torch orange, cleaner cage-bar color (baseline renders it as a garish flat magenta smear, post-off renders it as a believable wine-red), and floor noise drops from "TV static" to a calm surface. A post pass that is strictly worse than "off" in every comparison is a bug, not a style choice. Do the fix: cut dither dot opacity by at least half, remove the uniform "static" speckle layer (the sparse white dots scattered everywhere, visible in `ours/01-arena-wide.png`), and reduce the haze desaturation.

3. **1-px outlines don't read on dark surfaces (must_have violation).** Toggling `2` (outline) off vs on changes almost nothing on the unlit wall bricks — the outline color is close enough in value to the fill that it nearly disappears (compare `/tmp/look-inspect/wall-on.png` and `/tmp/look-inspect/wall-off.png`, both crops of the same dark wall region). Outlines read fine on the lit floor near a torch (crisp black against warm orange) but fail on exactly the surfaces that dominate the frame — most of every wall, and most of the floor away from a light pool. Darken/saturate the outline on unlit surfaces specifically, don't use one flat outline value for both lit and unlit fill.

4. **The floor is covered in a mechanical checkerboard/static pattern that reads as noise, not craft.** Visible across the whole floor in every capture (e.g. `ours/01-arena-wide.png`). It's a uniform, regular checker grid plus scattered single-pixel white specks — it doesn't vary with the scene, doesn't track light or shadow shape, and looks like an unfinished dither test pattern left on by default rather than a deliberate shading choice. GAME.md asks for "dithered or flat shading, never smooth gradients," which this technically is, but the reference shows what a *good* version of that constraint looks like: flat, confident color fields with dithering reserved for actual gradient edges (see `ref/01-arena-wide.png`'s floor, which reads as clean flat stone even though it's still low-color-count pixel art).

5. **The hero is easy to lose at a glance, including in normal play.** In `?scene=run&seed=1`, the hero spawns tucked into a dark alcove at the top-left of the first arena and is genuinely hard to spot on first look — same low-value blue against a similarly dark archway, no rim light or contrast boost separating him from the architecture (see `/tmp/look-inspect/run7/check2.png`). GAME.md's own bar is "Player, enemies, projectiles, and hazards must separate from the floor at a glance, even in a thumbnail" — the hero fails this in his own idle spawn pose in a piece whose whole job is render readability.

6. **The two creatures in the showcase vignette read as debris, not characters.** The showcase's own composition (`?showcase=look`) is "hero, two enemies, torches, props" — but the two non-hero creatures are a jumble of small gray articulated blocks and a lumpy maroon blob with no clear head/body/silhouette read (see `ours/03-lit-pool-detail.png`, bottom-right, and `ours/02-combat-closeup.png`). Whether or not the creature *models* are this piece's responsibility, the still frame this piece is judged on ("a still frame of an arena reads as a finished pixel-art game, not a tech demo") includes them, and right now they read as scrap piles, which drags the whole vignette toward "tech demo."

7. **Minor: room-name intro cards linger long enough to dominate the first several seconds of normal play.** In `?scene=run&seed=1`, "THE GAUNTLET / SEED 1" and then "ARENA 1/5 THE VESTIBULE" each stayed on screen, centered and high-contrast, for several real-time seconds with no player action shortening them in testing — during that whole window the arena's lighting and detail (this piece's subject) is upstaged by static text. Likely owned by run-flow/arenas rather than look, but it directly interferes with judging the render in normal play, so noting it here.

## must_have checklist

- **Pixels never swim or shimmer when the camera moves (texel snapping): PASS.** Diffed consecutive pan frames (`/tmp/look-inspect/pan3/t0.png` vs `t3000.png`) — the scene shifts in crisp, discrete steps with no blur or shimmer at any zoom level tested (checked at 640×360, 1280×720, and 1920×1080 capture sizes).
- **One palette used everywhere, UI included: PASS, with caveat.** In-game HUD (hearts, shard counter, dash pips) reads as palette-consistent with the environment. Not able to fully verify against `src/render/palette.js` since code isn't read per protocol; judged by eye only.
- **1-px outlines that read on both dark and lit surfaces: FAIL.** Reads on lit floor near torches; nearly disappears on unlit walls and shadowed floor, which is most of the frame. See Problem 3.
- **Flickering torch light that actually lights nearby voxels: FAIL.** The glow is a flat post-process decal on the floor plane; wall voxels next to a torch get zero brightness falloff with post off. Flicker itself is present (confirmed via repeated bursts) but it's flickering a decal, not a light. See Problem 1 / Biggest gap.
- **A still frame of an arena reads as a finished pixel-art game, not a tech demo: FAIL.** Noisy dither/haze, low-contrast outlines, weak hero/creature readability. The raw render with post off comes much closer to passing this than the shipped default does.
