PIN: https://pizzatowerguy.itch.io/pizza-tower-demo | 4.9 stars, 402 ratings | The signature mechanic of Pizza Tower is an "escape" sequence: a level-ending sprint away from a screen-filling pursuing threat through the same corridors you just explored, with fleeing enemies and speed-blur used to sell danger and momentum — the closest well-known, top-rated itch.io analogue to "chased by the collapse" gauntlet corridors. It is Windows-only (not browser-playable), so this critique uses the developer's own GIFs/screenshots from the itch page rather than live capture, per protocol.

## Verdict (not blind)

The reference wins decisively: it has a visible, expressive character selling the chase with body language and screen-filling danger, where ours has no visible player character at all during the very sequence this piece exists to deliver.

## Biggest gap

**The hero is completely invisible during corridor play**, in both `?showcase=gauntlet` and normal play (`/?scene=run` after `debug.goto` into a corridor segment). I moved the hero manually with WASD (confirmed via `window.__GR.state().hero.x/z` changing every frame) and it never appears on screen — not as a sprite, not as a silhouette, not even as a dust cloud. In the arena scene immediately before the corridor, the hero's blue-hooded voxel model is clearly visible (see `evidence/run1/run-start.png`, top-left). The instant `debug.goto(1)` enters the corridor, the character vanishes and never reappears in dozens of subsequent frames, including while taking a "buried by the collapse" hit. A corridor whose entire premise is "watch yourself dash and dodge while being chased" is unwatchable with no self to watch. Fix: whatever `corridor.js`/`showcase-gauntlet.js` does to the scene graph on entering a corridor (new camera rig? separate render layer? culling by room bounds?) is dropping the hero mesh. Get the hero rendering in the corridor with the same model, animation and camera-relative screen position it has in arenas, before touching anything else.

## Problems

1. **No visible player character in corridors at all (must_have failure, game-breaking).** See Biggest gap. Evidence: `evidence/run2/goto1.png`, `evidence/run3/settled.png`, `evidence/run3/moving-right.png`, `evidence/run4/moved-left.png`, `evidence/run4/moved-down.png`, `evidence/botrun/bot-*.png`, and the `ours/` blind captures — none show a hero, across god-mode, bot-mode, and manual keyboard control, across two different corridors and two different capture sessions. This alone makes trap fairness, dash timing, and "does this look tense" all unjudgeable by a player, because there is nothing to identify with on screen.

2. **The corridor's visual style breaks from the rest of the game's established voxel look.** Every other piece (arenas, look, hero, enemies — see `hurdles/waves/w1/critic/arenas/ours/01-room-still.png` and `hurdles/waves/w1/critic/look/ours/01-arena-wide.png`) renders chunky, per-vertex-AO voxels with visible outlines and a three-quarter camera that shows depth. The gauntlet corridor (`evidence/showcase1-play/start.png`, `evidence/showcase1/frame.png`) reads almost flat: wide rectangular floor/wall tile blocks with dot-dither noise, thin pink zigzag "spike" sprites, and flat cyan cross icons for vents. It looks like a different, cruder game bolted onto this one — the GAME.md bar of "chunky voxels ... baked per-vertex ambient occlusion and one-pixel dark outlines" is not met here even before the hero-visibility problem. Fix: run the corridor traps and collapse through the same voxel meshing/outline/AO pipeline `look` and `arenas` use, at the same apparent camera angle and zoom.

3. **HUD/debug text is dense and covers the play area even outside `debug=1`.** By default the showcase prints four lines of status text (`GAUNTLET 2/4  THE BURNING STAIR`, `CHASE 0.1S GAP 6.1 WALL 1.0 U/S`, the trap-order list, `BOT: RUN`) across the top ~80px of a 720px-tall frame, and a control-hint bar across the bottom. That's showcase-only instrumentation and fine for a showcase, but there is no clean "story" frame available without a debug overlay, which made building a fair still comparison harder than for other pieces. Not disqualifying on its own, but worth a toggle to hide it for capture purposes (the `H` key toggles the on-screen `debug=1` panel, but not this always-on status header).

4. **Tension ramp is plausible but thin.** `WALL u/s` (the collapse's speed) did rise over the course of a run (0.0 → 1.0 → 2.8–3.4 U/S as `CHASE`/`GAP` changed), consistent with "speeds up if you dawdle." I did not get a clean side-by-side of "player far ahead → wall visibly eases" because the bot always ran at a fixed pace; a critic (or player) currently has no easy way to deliberately lag or lead without fighting the bot/AI or scripting `debug` hooks. This must_have is not falsified, but it's also not clearly demonstrated in normal play.

5. **The release beat is a text pop-up, not an event.** Reaching the end shows cyan "ESCAPED" text with a time and "CLOSEST" stat (`evidence/botrun/bot-12.png`), and `WALL u/s` drops to 0. There is no door-slam, no shake, no visible change to the environment marking the transition — must_have explicitly asks for "door slams behind you, the rumble stops" as a release beat, and only the second half (rumble stops) is evidenced. Combined with problem #1 (no hero), the payoff of surviving a corridor currently has almost no visual weight.

6. **Trap telegraphing can only be verified through a debug overlay.** Pressing `T` shows red/gold timing bars over each trap ("TIMING: RED = HURTS  GOLD = WARNING", `evidence/showcase1-play/timing-overlay.png`), which is good instrumentation for a builder, but in normal, non-debug play the only telegraph is each trap's own animation (fire vents building glow before eruption, blades color-cycling). I could not confirm fairness on first sight from the outside because — again — there is no visible hero to judge reaction time against.

## must_have checklist

- "the collapse is spectacular: falling voxels, dust, rumbling shake, ember glow" — **partial pass**. The advancing wall (`evidence/run3/moving-right.png`, `evidence/botrun/bot-12.png`) does show a mass of falling ember/rock particles with glow, and it fills more of the screen as it closes in. It reads as busy/noisy rather than voxel-chunky (see problem #2), and I could not judge camera shake from static screenshots (no captured video/webm).
- "every trap telegraphs its timing and is fair on first sight" — **fail (unverifiable in normal play)**. Telegraphing exists but only confirmed via the `T` debug overlay; fairness can't be judged without a visible hero to check reaction windows against (problem #6, problem #1).
- "tension ramps: the collapse speeds up if you dawdle and eases if you are far ahead" — **partial pass**. Speed-up over time observed (problem #4); easing when ahead not demonstrated.
- "reaching the end has a release beat: door slams behind you, the rumble stops" — **fail**. Only a text readout and the wall halting; no door, no slam, no distinct visual event (problem #5).
