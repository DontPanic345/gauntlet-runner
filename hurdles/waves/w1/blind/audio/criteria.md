# Blind comparison: audio

**Question:** Which soundscape is more satisfying and coherent? Consider the music (is it composed, varied, and does it carry the moment?), the sound effects (do hits and attacks read clearly and feel good?), and how the two sit together in the mix.

**How to read each panel.** A judge cannot hear these games, so each panel is a picture of a recording of the game's full output (the master bus), captured losslessly in the browser:
- **Top:** a spectrogram. Time runs left to right, and pitch runs from low (bottom) to high (top, about 22 kHz). Brighter means louder. Horizontal lines are sustained pitched notes. Vertical bands are percussive or noisy events, such as drums, hits and explosions.
- **Middle:** a ruler with one tick per second.
- **Bottom:** the waveform on a linear scale. Taller means louder.

Both sides were recorded the same way, at the same viewport size, and are shown at the same duration within each pair. Overall loudness is closely matched (integrated loudness within about 2 LU in every pair), so the brightness of one panel against the other is not a volume trick. Thin full-height hairlines on either side may be glitches from the headless test browser, not the game, so do not count them. Ignore genre and instrument choice as such. Judge the quality, variety and clarity of the result.

- **Pair 01:** both panels: the first 10 s of sound a player hears when the game opens, with no further input (the title or opening screen running into whatever comes next).
- **Pair 02:** both panels: 10 s standing still in a combat room while enemies are present, with no attacks from the player (the background music and ambience of a fight, plus any enemy sounds).
- **Pair 03:** both panels: 10 s of active combat, with the player attacking enemies about every 0.3 to 0.4 s and sometimes dodging or moving.
- **Pair 04:** both panels: a 3 s close-up taken from the pair 03 recordings (seconds 3 to 6), with a finer time scale, so individual attacks and impacts can be seen against the music.

Written descriptions (neutral, from the critic's measurements):

- **A, pair 01:** a slow (about 76 bpm) arpeggio of bright, buzzy, chip-style pulse-wave chords, one note every eighth note, with a soft bass under it. Each note has a stack of evenly spaced harmonics that reaches to about 15 kHz. The level is almost constant (loudness range 0.3 LU).
- **B, pair 01:** a short descending whoosh and some dialogue blips, then a dense, full-band music track with a beat, sustained harmony and a melodic line. There is more variation in level (loudness range 2.2 LU).
- **A, pair 02:** sparse. Mostly a regular low drum pulse and short bass notes over a quiet drone, with occasional enemy sounds. There is no melody. The median level is about 5 dB below B's.
- **B, pair 02:** a continuous, dense music track, with harmony, melody and percussion filling the whole spectrum, at a steady level.
- **A, pair 03:** chip-style music with a drum, a bass and a busy pulse-wave arpeggio. Short, punchy hit and swing sounds stand well above the music: they peak about 16 dB above the median level.
- **B, pair 03:** the same dense music as pair 02, plus long, loud, broadband explosion bursts from the player's attacks, each about 0.3 to 0.5 s long, which fill the whole spectrum while they last. Peaks are about 8 dB above the median level.
- **Pair 04:** close-ups of the pair 03 recordings described above.
